`tree` is `ls`'s more visual cousin. Instead of a flat file listing, it
draws a directory's structure as an indented tree, complete with branch
lines connecting parents to their children — which makes it much
easier to build a mental map of an unfamiliar project at a glance than
scrolling through a plain listing.

It's purely a convenience tool: everything `tree` shows could in
principle be reconstructed from `find` or a recursive `ls`, just far
less readably. The most common adjustment people make is excluding
noisy folders like `node_modules` or `.git` with `-I`, since otherwise
a typical web project's tree output is dominated by dependency files
that aren't actually relevant to the structure you're trying to
understand.
