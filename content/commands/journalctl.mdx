`journalctl` queries the centralized log that `systemd` collects from every service it manages, giving you one consistent tool for reading logs instead of hunting through separate files for each service. It's the standard companion to `systemctl` — `status` tells you a service's current state, `journalctl` shows the full history of what it's logged.

`-u SERVICE` filters to just one service's entries, and `-f` follows the log live, the same idea as `tail -f` but for the structured systemd journal instead of a plain text file. `--since` narrows results to a time window, which matters once a service has been running long enough to accumulate a large amount of log history.
