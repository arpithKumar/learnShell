`netstat` shows you what's currently listening on which network ports,
and what active connections exist to and from your machine. The
classic use case is finding out "what's already using port 3000" right
before starting a dev server, or confirming a service is actually
listening where you expect it to be after starting it.

The `-an` combination — all connections, numeric addresses instead of
resolved hostnames — is the version most people reach for, since
hostname resolution can be slow and isn't usually necessary just to
check whether something is listening on a given port. Piping that
through `grep` for a specific port number turns a long, noisy table
into a single relevant line.

On Linux specifically, `netstat` has been gradually superseded by the
`ss` command, which is generally faster and provides more detail; on
macOS, `netstat` remains the standard built-in tool with no real
push toward a replacement.
