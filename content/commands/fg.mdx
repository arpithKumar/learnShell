`fg` is the opposite of `bg`: it takes a background or paused job and
attaches it back to your terminal as the foreground process, the same
as if you'd started it directly without the trailing `&`. This is handy
when something you backgrounded earlier now needs direct interaction —
maybe it's prompting for input, or you simply want to watch its output
attentively again instead of letting it run unattended.

In day-to-day use, `fg` and `bg` are mostly reached for as a pair: pause
something with `Ctrl+Z`, decide whether it should keep running quietly
in the background (`bg`) or actually needs your attention right now
(`fg`). Multiple jobs can be tracked at once via the numbers shown by
`jobs`, with `fg %2` bringing back a specific one instead of just the
most recent.
