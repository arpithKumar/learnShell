`openssl` is a large toolkit covering most everyday cryptography needs from the command line: generating private keys and certificates, computing hashes, encrypting and decrypting data, and inspecting live TLS connections to diagnose certificate problems.

For local development, `openssl req -x509 ...` generates a self-signed certificate, letting you test HTTPS locally without a real certificate authority. For debugging a production site, `openssl s_client -connect host:443` connects directly and prints the full certificate chain the server presents, which is often the fastest way to see exactly why a browser is complaining about a certificate.

Given how much ground it covers, nobody reasonably memorizes all of `openssl`'s subcommands and flags — looking up the exact syntax for whatever specific task is in front of you is the normal way to use it, not a sign of unfamiliarity.
