`cat` ("concatenate") is one of the very first commands most people
learn, and it does exactly what it sounds like: it dumps a file's
contents straight to your terminal, from start to end, with no
pagination or scrolling controls of its own.

Its real purpose, though, is hinted at by its name. Give `cat` more
than one file and it prints them one after another as a single
continuous stream — which is exactly what you want when joining files
together: `cat part1.txt part2.txt > combined.txt` merges two files
into a third in one step, with the `>` redirecting the combined output
into a new file instead of your screen.

For just reading a single file, `cat` is fine for short files, but it
has no way to scroll back once text has flown past — that's what `less`
is for. A useful mental rule: reach for `cat` when you want to *use* a
file's contents (print it, combine it, pipe it elsewhere), and `less`
when you want to *read* it.
