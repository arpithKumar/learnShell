`declare` creates a variable with explicit attributes attached, going beyond a plain assignment's default behavior of "just a string." With `-i`, a variable behaves as an integer, supporting arithmetic directly without the usual `$((...))` wrapper; with `-a`, it becomes an array; with `-r`, it becomes read-only, genuinely immutable for the rest of the script.

Most everyday shell variables don't need any of this — a plain `name=value` assignment is perfectly fine. `declare` is worth reaching for specifically when you need one of those particular behaviors, most commonly `-r` for defining a constant that shouldn't be accidentally overwritten later in a long script.
