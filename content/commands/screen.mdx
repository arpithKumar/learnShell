`screen` creates a terminal session that survives even after you disconnect — closing the SSH connection or terminal window doesn't kill the process running inside, since it keeps going in the background on the remote machine. Reattaching later with `screen -r` picks up exactly where you left off, scrollback and all.

This solves a real, common problem: starting a long build, download, or script on a remote server and not wanting it to die the moment a network connection drops. `tmux` is the more modern alternative with similar goals, often preferred for richer pane and window splitting, but `screen` remains widely available and simple for the core detach-and-reattach use case.
