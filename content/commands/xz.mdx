`xz` is the newest of the common single-file compression tools, and generally achieves the smallest output of the three — smaller than both `gzip` and `bzip2` — at the cost of being the slowest to compress. It's a reasonable default when distributing software for download, where a smaller file matters more than the compression step being fast.

Usage follows the same pattern as `gzip` and `bzip2`: compress in place, `-d` to decompress, `-k` to keep the original. With `tar`, it's selected via the capital `-J` flag (`tar -cJf archive.tar.xz ./folder`).
