`history` lists the commands you've already typed in the current shell session, each with a number you can use to reference it again (`!503` reruns command #503, for example). It's a quick way to look back at what you just did, or to find a long command you don't want to retype.

In day-to-day use, most people interact with history through `Ctrl+R` rather than typing `history` directly — it searches backward through the same list interactively as you type, which is usually faster than scrolling a printed list looking for one specific line.
