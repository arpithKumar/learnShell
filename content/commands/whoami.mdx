`whoami` prints exactly one thing: the username of the account you're currently running commands as. It's a small, single-purpose command, most useful as a quick sanity check after switching users with `su` or running something with `sudo -u`, confirming you're actually operating as the account you meant to be.

For anything beyond just the username — group memberships, numeric user and group IDs — `id` is the more complete tool.
