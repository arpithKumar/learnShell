Where `du` answers "how much space does this particular folder use,"
`df` answers a different, bigger-picture question: "how much space is
left on this entire drive?" Run with no arguments, it lists every
mounted filesystem along with its total size, how much is used, how
much is free, and the percentage full.

`df -h` is the version almost everyone actually types — the `-h` makes
sizes human-readable (gigabytes instead of raw block counts), which is
the difference between an instantly scannable table and a column of
numbers you'd have to do mental math on. It's the fastest first check
when something starts failing with a vague "no space left on device"
error.
