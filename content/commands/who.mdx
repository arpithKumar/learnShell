`who` lists everyone currently logged in to the system, along with which terminal they're connected through and when their session started. It's most relevant on shared or remote machines, where more than one person might be logged in at the same time.

The related but more detailed `w` command builds on the same information, adding what each user is currently doing — useful when "who's logged in" isn't quite enough and you also want "and what are they running."
