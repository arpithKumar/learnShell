While `ps` gives you a single snapshot, `top` gives you a live,
auto-refreshing dashboard of every running process, sorted by whichever
resource matters most — usually CPU or memory by default. It's the
natural first step when a machine feels sluggish and you want to know
exactly what's responsible.

Running `top` takes over your whole terminal until you exit, which can
be disorienting the first time — it's not a normal command that prints
output and returns; it's an interactive, full-screen display that keeps
running and updating until you explicitly quit it (typically by
pressing `q`).

Once it's open, the columns to watch are usually `%CPU` and `%MEM` (or
similarly named columns depending on your system) next to each
process's command name. A process pinned near 100% CPU, or one whose
memory usage keeps climbing without leveling off, are both classic
signs worth investigating further with `ps` or by checking the
program's own logs.
