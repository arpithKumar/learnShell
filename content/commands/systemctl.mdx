`systemctl` controls services managed by `systemd`, the service manager used by most modern Linux distributions — starting, stopping, restarting, and checking the status of background services like web servers, databases, and daemons.

`systemctl status SERVICE` is the command worth knowing best: it shows whether a service is active, when it last started or stopped, and a few of its most recent log lines, all in one view — usually the fastest way to begin diagnosing why something isn't behaving as expected. `enable` is the separate step that makes a service start automatically on boot, distinct from just starting it for the current session.
