`unzip` extracts the contents of a `.zip` archive, the counterpart to `zip`. Run with no flags, it unpacks everything into the current directory and asks before overwriting anything that already exists — a small but genuinely useful safety net that `tar` doesn't offer by default.

`-l` lists an archive's contents without touching the filesystem at all, which is the safe way to check what you're about to extract. `-d` lets you choose a destination folder instead of dumping files into wherever you happen to be standing.
