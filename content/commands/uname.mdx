`uname` ("Unix name") prints basic facts about the system it's running on: the kernel name, version, and hardware architecture. It's a quick first check when you need to know exactly what platform you're working with, especially before installing software that has architecture-specific builds.

`uname -a` dumps everything at once, which is the version most people actually run. For scripts, the individual flags are more useful — `-m` for architecture (handy for distinguishing Apple Silicon from Intel Macs, or x86 from ARM Linux), `-s` for just the OS name, `-r` for the kernel version.
