`groups` lists every group a user belongs to, which is often the fastest way to understand why a user can or can't do something on a system — many permissions, like running Docker or accessing certain devices, are granted through group membership rather than individual user permissions.

It overlaps with the group information shown by `id`, but `groups` gives a plain list with nothing else mixed in, useful for a quick check before digging deeper into a specific permission error.
