`gpg` (GNU Privacy Guard) implements public-key cryptography for encrypting files, verifying signatures, and proving a message or file genuinely came from a specific person unaltered. It's the open-source standard behind PGP-style encrypted email and signed software releases.

The core idea is a key pair, like SSH's: a public key you share freely, and a private key you guard absolutely. Anyone can encrypt a message *for* you using your public key, but only your private key can decrypt it — meaning you never need to share anything secret just to receive encrypted messages. Signing works in the complementary direction: you sign something with your private key, and anyone with your public key can verify the signature is genuinely yours and the content hasn't been tampered with.

Because there's no recovery path for a lost private key by design — that's what makes the whole system trustworthy — backing it up securely is a real, non-optional responsibility once you start relying on GPG for anything important.
