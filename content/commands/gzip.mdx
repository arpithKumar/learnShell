`gzip` compresses a single file, replacing it with a `.gz` version and removing the original — it's a one-file-at-a-time tool, unlike `tar`, which bundles many files together first. That's exactly why the two are so often combined: `tar` collects multiple files into one, and `gzip` (via `tar`'s `-z` flag) shrinks the result.

Used standalone, `gzip somefile.txt` compresses it into `somefile.txt.gz` and deletes the plain version — if you want to keep both, pass `-k`. Decompressing is the mirror image, either with `gzip -d` or its dedicated counterpart, `gunzip`.

Compression level is adjustable from `-1` (fastest, larger output) to `-9` (slowest, smallest output), though the default usually strikes a reasonable balance without needing to think about it.
