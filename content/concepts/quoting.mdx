Quoting tells the shell how literally to treat a piece of text. Without quotes, the shell applies word-splitting (breaking on spaces) and globbing (expanding `*` and similar wildcards) to whatever you type, which is rarely what you actually want once a value can contain spaces or special characters.

Double quotes (`"..."`) suppress word-splitting and globbing but still allow `$variable` and `$(command)` expansion inside the string — the safe, usual choice. Single quotes (`'...'`) suppress everything, including variable expansion, making the text completely literal — useful for things like a regex pattern you don't want the shell touching before it reaches `grep` or `sed`. Forgetting to quote a variable that might contain a space is the single most common way a working-looking script breaks on real-world input.
