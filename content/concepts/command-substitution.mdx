Command substitution runs a command and replaces `$(command)` with whatever that command printed to standard output — letting you weave a command's result directly into another command, a variable assignment, or a string. It's one of the most-used pieces of shell syntax, since it's the standard way to capture a value computed by some other tool.

The older backtick syntax, `` `command` ``, does the same thing but is harder to nest (each level needs backslash-escaping) and visually easy to confuse with a regular quote mark — `$(...)` is the modern, POSIX-standardized replacement and is almost always the better choice in new scripts.
