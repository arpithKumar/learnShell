Word splitting is the step where an unquoted expansion's result gets broken into separate words, using the characters in the `IFS` (Internal Field Separator) variable as delimiters — by default, space, tab, and newline. This is exactly why an unquoted variable holding `"my file.txt"` becomes two separate arguments instead of one: the space inside it is treated as a word boundary, identical to typing two separate words yourself.

Wrapping an expansion in double quotes suppresses word splitting entirely, which is why "always quote your variables" is such a strong, simple rule of thumb — it sidesteps this mechanism altogether rather than requiring you to reason about `IFS` in the common case. Deliberately changing `IFS` (commonly to a comma, for parsing CSV-like data) is a real, useful technique, just one that needs care since it affects every unquoted expansion until it's reset.
