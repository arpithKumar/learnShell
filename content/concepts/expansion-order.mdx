The shell doesn't perform all its text-transformation tricks in one undifferentiated pass — it applies them in a fixed sequence: brace expansion first, then tilde expansion, then parameter/variable expansion, command substitution, and arithmetic expansion (these three together, left to right), then word splitting, then pathname expansion (globbing), and finally quote removal at the very end.

Knowing this order explains otherwise-confusing results, like why `{1..$n}` doesn't expand a range based on a variable — brace expansion runs *before* parameter expansion, so `$n` is still literal, unexpanded text at the moment braces are processed, and the whole thing falls through unchanged. This sequence is explicitly documented in bash's manual rather than being inferred behavior, which makes it worth treating as a reliable fact to reason from rather than a rule of thumb.
