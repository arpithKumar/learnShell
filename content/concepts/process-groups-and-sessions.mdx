Processes aren't just a flat list of independent PIDs — they're organized into process groups (commonly, every process in one pipeline shares a group) and sessions (typically, everything started from one terminal login). This hierarchy is what makes job control actually work: pressing `Ctrl-C` sends `SIGINT` to the entire foreground process group at once, which is why a multi-stage pipeline you interrupt stops as a whole, not just its first command.

This distinction matters practically when trying to stop something more complex than a single process — `kill PID` only reaches one process, while `kill -- -PID` (note the leading dash) targets the entire process group that PID is the leader of. A session can hold several process groups simultaneously, but only one is ever the active foreground group receiving terminal-generated signals at any given moment; the rest run in the background, insulated from `Ctrl-C` entirely.
