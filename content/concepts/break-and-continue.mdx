`break` exits a loop immediately, skipping any remaining iterations entirely — useful once you've found what you were looking for and don't need to keep checking the rest. `continue` instead skips just the current iteration and jumps straight to the next one, useful for "skip this case but keep going" logic without wrapping the rest of the loop body in an `if`.

Both work inside `for`, `while`, and `until` loops alike, and both accept an optional numeric argument for breaking or continuing an outer loop from inside a nested one — `break 2` exits two levels of nesting at once rather than just the innermost loop.
