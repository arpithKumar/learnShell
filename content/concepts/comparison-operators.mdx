Shell tests split comparison into two separate sets of operators depending on what you're comparing. For strings: `=` (or `==` inside `[[ ]]`) for equality, `!=` for inequality. For integers: `-eq`, `-ne`, `-lt`, `-gt`, `-le`, `-ge` — spelled-out abbreviations rather than symbols, since `<` and `>` were already taken by redirection and `=` was already taken by string comparison by the time numeric tests needed their own syntax.

Mixing the two up is a genuinely easy mistake with a quiet failure mode: `[ "$x" = "10" ]` checks whether `$x` is the literal text `"10"`, not whether it numerically equals ten — `"10"` and `"10.0"` or `"010"` would all fail that check despite being the same number. Reaching for `-eq` whenever the comparison is conceptually about numbers, not text, avoids this category of bug entirely.
