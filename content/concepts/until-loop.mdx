`until` repeats a block of commands for as long as its condition keeps failing, stopping the moment the condition finally succeeds — the exact mirror image of `while`, which repeats for as long as the condition keeps succeeding. `until [ -f ready.txt ]; do sleep 1; done` is a simple polling pattern: keep waiting while the file doesn't exist yet, stop once it does.

It's mostly a readability choice rather than a capability `while` lacks entirely — anything written with `until` could also be written as `while` with the condition negated. But for conditions that read more naturally as "keep going until X happens" rather than "keep going while NOT X," `until` avoids the double-negative phrasing and is usually the clearer choice.
