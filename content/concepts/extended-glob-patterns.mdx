Plain globbing (`*`, `?`, `[abc]`) can match characters and runs of characters, but it has no way to express ideas like "one or more repetitions" or "anything except this." Extended glob patterns fill that gap once enabled with `shopt -s extglob`: `+(pattern)` matches one or more occurrences, `*(pattern)` matches zero or more, `?(pattern)` matches zero or one, `@(pattern)` matches exactly one of several alternatives, and `!(pattern)` matches anything that *doesn't* match.

This is a bash-specific option, off by default, and meaningfully more powerful than plain globbing for filtering filenames by a more precise shape — separating numbered files from non-numbered ones, for instance, in a way plain wildcards genuinely can't express. It still isn't full regular-expression power, though; for cases extended globs can't quite reach, `=~` regex matching inside `[[ ]]` is the next step up.
