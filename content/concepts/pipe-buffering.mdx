A pipe isn't an unlimited conduit — the kernel gives it a fixed-size buffer (commonly 64KB on Linux), and once that buffer fills up, the writing process blocks (pauses) until the reading process drains some of it. This is exactly why `yes | sleep 100` doesn't spin forever generating infinite output: `yes` writes as fast as it can until the buffer is full, then simply waits, using essentially no CPU, since nothing is reading from the other end.

This buffering behavior is part of why pipeline output sometimes seems to lag — many tools buffer their own writes in larger chunks specifically when writing to a pipe (as opposed to an interactive terminal, where they often flush line by line), which can make a long pipeline appear to pause and then dump a burst of output all at once rather than streaming smoothly.
