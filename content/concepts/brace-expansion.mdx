Brace expansion generates multiple literal strings from one compact pattern — `{1..3}` becomes `1 2 3`, and `{a,b,c}` becomes `a b c` — entirely as a text-generation step, before the shell even considers globbing or running anything. `mkdir project/{src,tests,docs}` is a common real use: one command creating three sibling directories at once.

It's easy to confuse with globbing since both use special characters to "expand" into multiple things, but they're conceptually different: globbing matches existing files on disk and produces nothing if there's no match, while brace expansion is pure text generation that doesn't look at the filesystem at all and always produces its full expansion. It's also a bash/zsh feature, not part of strict POSIX `sh`.
