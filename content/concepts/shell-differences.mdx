Bash, zsh, dash/sh, and fish are all "shells," but they differ meaningfully in syntax and feature support. Bash and zsh share most extensions (arrays, brace expansion, `[[ ]]`) and are largely interchangeable for everyday scripting; strict POSIX `sh` (often actually `dash` under the hood) supports only the more minimal, standardized feature set, lacking arrays and several conveniences bash scripts take for granted; fish deliberately abandons POSIX syntax compatibility entirely in favor of its own, different design.

The shebang line declares *intent*, not a guarantee — a script saying `#!/bin/bash` will only work correctly if bash is actually installed at that path, and a script saying `#!/bin/sh` needs to avoid every bash-only feature, since `/bin/sh` might be a genuinely more limited shell on a given system. Most real-world confusion in this area comes from writing and testing a script under bash, then having it unexpectedly run under a stricter `/bin/sh` elsewhere — knowing this category of risk exists is most of the battle.
