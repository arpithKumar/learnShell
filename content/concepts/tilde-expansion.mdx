A `~` at the start of a word expands to a home directory: plain `~` expands to your own (`$HOME`), and `~username` expands to that specific user's home directory. It's purely a convenience the shell performs before running anything — `cd ~/projects` is really just shorthand for `cd /home/alice/projects`, substituted in before `cd` ever sees the argument.

This only applies when `~` is the very first character of a word — `path/~/file` doesn't expand, since the tilde isn't leading. A couple of less common variants exist too: `~+` for the current directory and `~-` for the previous one, both maintained automatically by the shell as you `cd` around.
