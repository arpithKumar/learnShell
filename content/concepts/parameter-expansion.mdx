Parameter expansion is the shell's built-in syntax for transforming a variable's value as part of expanding it — applying a default, removing a prefix or suffix, or measuring its length — all inline with `${...}`, without needing to pipe the value through a separate command like `sed` or `cut`.

The most commonly reached-for forms are `${var:-default}` (use a fallback value if `var` is unset or empty, without changing `var` itself) and suffix/prefix removal like `${file%.txt}` (strip `.txt` from the end) or `${file#prefix}` (strip a prefix from the start). These read awkwardly at first compared to a more verbose explicit script, but they're standard, portable, and avoid spawning an extra process just to manipulate a string.
