Arithmetic expansion evaluates an integer math expression and substitutes the result, written as `$((expression))`. Without it, the shell has no concept of numbers at all — `$x + $y` where both hold digits is just string concatenation with literal `+` characters in the middle, not addition, which is exactly why this special syntax exists.

It supports the usual operators (`+ - * / %`), comparison, and even basic bitwise operations, but strictly as integers — there's no floating-point support built into the shell itself. Before `$((...))` was standardized, scripts had to call the external `expr` command for every calculation, which is slower (a whole extra process per operation) and part of why `expr` is now considered legacy outside a few specific use cases.
